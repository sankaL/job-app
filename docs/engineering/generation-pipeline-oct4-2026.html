The flow

Initial generation and full regeneration

Teal is the normal path. Orange is the uncommon path: escalation, fallback and repair. Steps appear in the order they run.

Request

frontend → backend → Redis queue

The user starts a generation. The backend reserves one writing request from the user's monthly quota, freezes the reviewed source snapshot, and enqueues the job. Profile contact details never leave the backend.

Prepare

worker · up to 20 jobs at once

The worker loads the section document, masks profile values in everything sent outbound, keeps fixed sections (Education, Certifications, and Skills at Low) local, and opens a budget of 10 model requests, 64k output tokens and a 240 s deadline (120 s for one section).

Round 0: two writer calls run at the same timeShared source and job description sit behind a prompt-cache breakpoint

Group 1 · Professional Experience

Writeresume_writer Sonnet 5.5, reasoning capped at 2k tokens. About 11–17 s; this group sets the total time.
Check locallySchema, same-role citations, numbers, employers and credentials, title rules, contact patterns. No model involved.
Jev claim auditclaim_audit One Decisions question per bullet and per renamed role title, 6 per batch, run in parallel. About 0.2–1 s.
Escalate uncertain claimsaudit_escalation Only the uncertain claims go to Sonnet, with Gemini as fallback. About 2–5 s, and often skipped.
Stream to the previewVerified sections go out as partial_sections on progress events.

Group 2 · Summary, Skills and other sections

Writeresume_writer Sonnet 5.5 · about 10–13 s.
Check locallySummary always cites its own source. Summary and Skills may cite Experience bullets.
Jev claim auditOne question per Summary sentence and per Skills group.
Escalate uncertain claimsSummary sentences escalate most often (1–2 per resume at Medium). The Sonnet check finishes while Group 1 is still writing, so it adds no time.
Stream to the previewThis group usually appears first, at about 12–14 s.
Repair rounds 1–2 (only for failed sections)

repair_writer Sonnet 5.5 rewrites only the failed sections, using the rejected output and the issue codes as feedback. Round 2 switches to GPT 6.1 Sol. A round starts only when its writes plus one audit still fit the 10-request budget. In the latest live runs, no repairs were needed.

Keep original instead of failing

A section that still can't be verified keeps its original text (source, or the current draft for full regeneration). It is flagged kept_original_unverified, and the workbench shows "Kept your original wording." until the user edits it. Generation fails only when every writable section would be kept.

Assemble and recheck

worker

A length check (an over-cap draft triggers one targeted trim), then a deterministic recheck of the whole document. Sections that fail the recheck also fall back to their originals. Contact details are reattached locally.

Deliver

Redis cache + callback → backend → UI

The result is cached before the success state is announced. The backend persists the draft and records attempts and kept_original_sections in usage events. The preview is replaced by the editable draft.

Grounding audit

How Jev decides, and when Sonnet steps in

Each claim's evidence is the text it cites plus the whole reviewed role it belongs to. Jev returns a probability for each option. Routing uses the probability of the passing option.

Claim typeMedium and Low optionsHigh optionsAccept whenReject whenOtherwise
Bullet, Summary sentence, Skills groupsupported plus issue codes (scope, metric, technology, employer, credential, date/tenure)plausible, implausible_claim, employer, credential, date/tenureP(pass) ≥ 0.80P(pass) ≤ 0.20Escalate to Sonnet
Renamed role titleacceptable_reframe or unsupported_role_reframe, worded for each level's title ruleP ≥ 0.25P ≤ 0.15Whole section to Sonnet

Measured accuracy

  • Labelled set of 684 claims. Medium: 99.2% of invented claims caught, 0% of good claims rejected, 1.2% escalated.
  • High: 96.6% caught, 9.5% of good claims rejected, 7.6% escalated.
  • Renamed titles (16 scenarios): every seniority bump and change of field rejected, all scoring ≤ 0.09.

What changes at High

  • Plausible job-fit additions (tools, scope, outcomes, metrics) are allowed when they fit the role, seniority and field.
  • Employers, dates, tenure, credentials, education and seniority are never invented at any level.
  • The local number check skips new non-year figures. Calendar years stay bound to the source.

Failure handling

What happens when something goes wrong

EventBehaviourUser sees
Primary model errors or times outThat call retries once on the role's fallback model. The other writer group is unaffected.Nothing, apart from a little extra time
Jev unavailable or returns invalid answersThe full LLM audit (Sonnet, then Gemini) checks every sectionNothing; audit takes ~5–10 s instead of ~0.5 s
Local check or audit rejects a sectionTargeted repair with issue codes, up to 2 roundsSlightly longer progress
Section still unverifiable after repairsOriginal text kept and flagged (initial and full regeneration)Kept your original wording.
Request budget or deadline exhaustedRemaining sections kept as originals if any section was tailored; otherwise the job fails with the real attempt countKept-original notice, or a retry message
Provider authentication or billing rejectionNo fallback; the job fails and the quota reservation is refundedGeneration failed with Retry
Telemetry (LangSmith) outageIgnored; never changes the resultNothing
Observability and limits

Where to look and what bounds the system

LangSmith runs

  • applix.resume_generation: workflow root with counts and outcome
  • applix.section_generation.pydantic_ai: one per writer group, with the serving provider, cost, reasoning settings and cached tokens
  • applix.section_grounding_audit: chain with applix.jev_audit.decisions children and any LLM escalation
  • Prompt and output bodies appear only when LANGSMITH_TRACE_CONTENT=true, and are redacted

Limits

  • Per workflow: 10 model requests (Jev not counted), 64k output tokens, 240 s or 120 s deadline
  • Per call: 16k output tokens; LLM audit 45 s; Jev 3 s with one retry
  • Worker: 20 concurrent jobs, at most 4 extraction browsers
  • Twenty simultaneous generations can reserve about $7–8 of OpenRouter in-flight credit, so keep the balance comfortable
